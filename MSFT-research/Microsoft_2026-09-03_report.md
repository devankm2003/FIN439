# Microsoft: Prior-Year Promises vs. Outcomes

## FY2023: What management said, and what followed

Microsoft described AI as a company-wide strategic priority and said it was building AI-enabled products, services, and infrastructure. It also warned that cloud and AI investment could raise operating costs and reduce margins. [Microsoft FY2023 Form 10-K, Item 1 and Item 7](https://microsoft.gcs-web.com/static-files/e2931fdb-9823-4130-b2a8-f6b8db0b15a9)

That strategy was followed by FY2024 Microsoft Cloud revenue of **$137.4 billion**, up **23%**, and **30%** growth in Azure and other cloud services. [Microsoft FY2024 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 39](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000095017024087843/msft-20240630.htm)

FY2024 management still warned that cloud and AI infrastructure investment would increase costs and could reduce operating margins as Microsoft expanded datacenters and server capacity for AI demand. [Microsoft FY2024 Form 10-K, Item 7, p. 40](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000095017024087843/msft-20240630.htm)

## FY2024: What management said, and what followed

Microsoft said it was helping customers obtain more value from Microsoft Cloud, leading the AI platform wave, and investing in generative-AI capabilities across consumer and commercial offerings. [Microsoft FY2024 Form 10-K, Item 1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000095017024087843/msft-20240630.htm)

It also disclosed that building and operating AI models, services, platforms, and infrastructure would require significant development and operating costs. [Microsoft FY2024 Form 10-K, Item 1A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000095017024087843/msft-20240630.htm)

In FY2025, Microsoft Cloud revenue grew **23%** to **$168.9 billion**; Azure and other cloud services grew **34%**; Microsoft 365 Commercial cloud revenue grew **15%**; and Dynamics 365 revenue grew **19%**. [Microsoft FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 33 and 37–38](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000095017025100235/msft-20250630.htm)

The cost warning also occurred: Microsoft Cloud gross margin was **69%**, with the decline attributed to scaling AI infrastructure, and Intelligent Cloud cost of revenue increased **36%**, driven by Azure growth. [Microsoft FY2025 Form 10-K, Item 7, pp. 36 and 38](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/789019/000095017025100235/msft-20250630.htm)

## Recent company evidence

Microsoft's July 29, 2026 FY26 Q4 investor-relations release reported FY2026 revenue of **$331.8 billion** (+18%), operating income of **$155.2 billion** (+21%), Microsoft Cloud revenue above **$214 billion** (+27%), Azure revenue above **$100 billion**, and more than **30 million** paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats. [Microsoft FY26 Q4 press release](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast)

On the same day's earnings call, CEO Satya Nadella said Microsoft added **31 datacenters** in the quarter and aimed to roughly double overall capacity in two years. CFO Amy Hood said FY2026 capital expenditures were **$41 billion**, about two-thirds of which was for short-lived assets, primarily CPUs and GPUs. [Microsoft FY26 Q4 earnings-call transcript](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4)

## Evidence-based read

The cited results show that Microsoft executed on its cloud and AI expansion: cloud revenue, Azure revenue, and AI-product monetization continued to grow. The same cited filings and call show that scaling AI requires substantial infrastructure spending and has weighed on gross-margin percentage. This is an inference from the cited evidence, not a factual claim.
